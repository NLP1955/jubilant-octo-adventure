## SECTION 1: HOW THE INDUSTRY WORKS — THE BASICS (0:30–2:30)
*Show: graphic illustrating the surplus fund recovery company model*

"Let me start by explaining what surplus fund recovery companies actually do, because there's a lot of confusion out there.

Surplus fund recovery companies — sometimes called overages recovery companies or foreclosure overages companies — are businesses that specialize in identifying surplus funds that are owed to former property owners, then helping those owners claim them.

Here's how the model works in a legitimate company:

**Step 1: Research.** The company searches public records — court filings, tax auction records, clerk databases — to find cases where a surplus was generated and where the funds are still unclaimed. This requires access to databases, knowledge of court systems in multiple states, and a lot of time.

**Step 2: Contact the Former Owner.** When a case is identified, the company reaches out to the former owner — or their heirs — to let them know funds exist and offer to help recover them.

**Step 3: Sign a Contract.** If the former owner agrees to work with the company, they sign a contract that specifies the company's fee — which in a legitimate company is always a contingency percentage. Meaning the company gets paid only if the money is recovered.

**Step 4: File the Claim.** The company gathers documentation, coordinates legal resources if needed, and files the claim with the appropriate court or county office.

**Step 5: Receive and Distribute Funds.** The surplus funds are released to the claimant, and the company takes its agreed percentage. The former owner receives the rest.

That's the legitimate model. Simple. Transparent. Risk-free for the former owner.

Now let me show you where it goes wrong."

## SECTION 2: RED FLAGS — WHAT TO AVOID (2:30–5:30)
*Show: red flag graphic with list — bold, clear visuals*

"I want you to watch for these red flags. Some of these are legal but unethical. Some of them are outright scams. Either way — they cost you money.

**Red Flag #1: Upfront Fees**
Any company that asks you to pay something before they recover your money is a company you should walk away from. Period. In legitimate surplus fund recovery, the company bears all the risk — not you. If they need payment before they start work, ask yourself why. Are they even confident they can recover anything?

Some companies ask for 'filing fees,' 'research fees,' 'processing fees.' Walk away.

**Red Flag #2: Excessive Percentage Fees**
The industry range for contingency fees varies. Some companies operate at reasonable rates. Others charge 30%, 40%, even 50% of the recovery. On a $100,000 surplus, that's $50,000 — gone. Before you sign anything, understand exactly what percentage you'll be giving up.

At NLP Sales, we believe in being fair to our clients. We discuss our fee structure with you openly during consultation, and we work hard to make sure you're walking away with as much of your money as possible.

**Red Flag #3: High-Pressure Tactics**
'You need to sign tonight or someone else will claim it.' 'If you don't respond in 24 hours, we're moving on.' This is pressure sales, and it's a warning sign. A legitimate company will give you time to ask questions, review the contract, and make an informed decision.

**Red Flag #4: No Attorney Involvement for Complex Cases**
If a company tells you that your case involves probate or competing claimants — but they say you don't need an attorney at all — be cautious. In cases that require legal representation, a proper recovery company will coordinate with licensed attorneys. A company that tells you no attorney is needed when one clearly is? They may be trying to cut corners, which can jeopardize your claim.

**Red Flag #5: No Physical Address or Verifiable Business Presence**
Some operations in this space are essentially one person with a website and no accountability. Before you work with any company, verify: Do they have a real address? Can you find them online beyond their own website? Are there reviews? Is there a real human being you can call?

We're at 7028 West Waters Avenue, Tampa, Florida. You can walk in. You can meet us. We're real.

**Red Flag #6: They Found You Through Suspicious Channels**
If someone contacts you claiming to know about your surplus funds and demanding immediate payment or a quick signature — before you've had any chance to verify the claim yourself — be suspicious. A good company reaches out, provides information, and invites you to verify through your own research. They don't rush you.

**Red Flag #7: No Clear Contract**
You should always receive a written agreement before any company begins working on your claim. That agreement should clearly state the percentage, what services will be provided, and the terms of payment. No contract? No deal."

*Show: green checkmark checklist graphic*

"Okay, so now let's flip it. What should a trustworthy surplus fund recovery company look like? Here's my checklist:

**Contingency-only fee structure.** No upfront costs. The company gets paid only when you get paid.

**Transparent fee disclosure.** The percentage is stated clearly, in writing, before you sign.

**Licensed attorney network.** For cases that require legal representation, a legitimate company has attorneys ready to step in — without additional upfront cost to you.

**Mobile notary service.** A company that's serious about serving you will make the document process convenient. You shouldn't have to drive to an office or courthouse to sign paperwork.

**Verifiable track record.** Look for testimonials, reviews, case examples. Ask the company about their experience. A good company will be happy to answer.

**Real physical presence.** An address. A phone number that a real person answers. An email that gets a response.

**No pressure.** They give you time to think. They answer your questions patiently. They explain the process clearly. They don't rush you.

**Communication throughout the process.** You should know what's happening with your case. A legitimate company keeps you informed.
