## SECTION 2: WHEN AN ATTORNEY IS REQUIRED (2:00–4:30)
*Show: graphic listing the legal scenarios that require attorney involvement*

"There are several situations in surplus fund cases that require an attorney to be involved. Here are the main ones:

**1. Probate is required.**
If the former property owner has passed away and no estate has been opened, you will need to go through probate before you can claim the surplus as an heir. Probate is a court process, and in most states — including Florida — it must be handled by a licensed attorney. You cannot represent an estate in court without a law license. Period.

**2. Competing claimants need to be resolved.**
If there are other parties claiming the surplus — a second mortgage holder, an HOA, a judgment creditor — and those disputes need to be litigated or mediated, an attorney must be involved. A judge isn't going to hear competing legal arguments from non-lawyers.

**3. Errors in the foreclosure case need to be challenged.**
Occasionally, there are issues with how the foreclosure was handled — improper notice, title defects, or other complications — that need to be addressed in court before the surplus can be released. That's legal work, and it requires an attorney.

**4. The surplus is being held in a complex trust or corporate structure.**
If the former owner was a business entity — an LLC, a corporation, a trust — and the claim needs to be made on behalf of that entity, it often requires formal legal action.

So yes, there are real situations where you need an attorney. And in every single one of those cases, NLP Sales has a solution — which I'll explain in a moment.
