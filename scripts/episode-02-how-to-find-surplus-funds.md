## SECTION 1: WHERE SURPLUS FUNDS ARE HELD (0:30–2:30)
*Show: `images/surplus-funds-series-cards.png` and `images/nlp-daily-report-collage.png`*

"Before you can find surplus funds, you need to understand where they're held. And this varies by state — which is one of the reasons so many people never find them.

**In Florida:**
The surplus from a tax deed sale is held by the county clerk of court. You can access the list through the county's official website or by visiting the clerk's office in person.

**In most states, there are two types of surplus funds:**

1. **Tax deed overages** — when a county sells a property for back taxes and collects more than what was owed.
2. **Foreclosure overages** — when a lender forecloses and the sale generates more than the mortgage balance.

Both are recoverable. Both require you to file paperwork. And both have deadlines you cannot miss.

Here's something most people don't know: **66% of former property owners never attend their own tax deed sale.** They don't even know their property was sold until it's too late. If that's your situation, you still have time — but you need to act now."

## SECTION 2: SEARCHING FOR SURPLUS FUNDS — FLORIDA STEP BY STEP (2:30–6:00)
*Screen recording of actual county website search*

"Let me walk you through exactly how to check in Florida. I'll use Hillsborough County as the example, but every county follows the same basic process.

**Step 1: Go to the county clerk's official website.**
For Hillsborough County, that's hillsclerk.com. Every Florida county has a similar portal.

**Step 2: Look for 'Tax Deed' or 'Surplus Funds' in the menu.**
You're looking for 'Tax Deed Surplus' or sometimes listed as 'Unclaimed Funds from Tax Deed Sales.'

**Step 3: Search by name.**
Type in your name, or the name of a deceased relative. The list will show all properties with unclaimed surplus funds attached to that name.

**Step 4: Note the case number.**
If you find a match, write down the case number, the property address, and the estimated surplus amount.

**Step 5: Check the deadline.**
Florida requires you to file a motion with the court to claim the funds. There are strict deadlines — act immediately.

**Pro tip:** If you find your name on the list, call NLP Sales before you do anything else. We'll tell you exactly what to do — for free — and we handle all the paperwork if you want us to."

## SECTION 3: SEARCHING IN OTHER STATES (6:00–8:30)
*Show the state lookup tool on the website*

"Now if you're not in Florida, here's how to search in the most active states:

**Texas:** Go to your county tax assessor-collector website. Search under 'Excess Proceeds from Tax Sales.' Remember — you only have 2 years.

**Georgia:** Search the Superior Court Clerk's website under 'Excess Funds.' Many Georgia counties post public lists.

**California:** Contact your county tax collector directly. Ask for 'Excess Proceeds' from tax sales. You only have 1 year.

**North Carolina:** Contact the county finance office. NC is one of the most claimant-friendly states — no hard deadline in most counties.

**Ohio:** Check the county auditor or common pleas court website. Ohio is also very claimant-friendly.

For any state, the general approach is:
1. Find the county clerk, tax collector, or circuit court for the county where the property was located.
2. Search for 'surplus funds', 'excess proceeds', or 'unclaimed tax sale funds.'
3. Search by name or by property address.
4. Note the amount and the deadline.
5. Call us — we'll tell you if it's worth pursuing and handle the claim."
